Creatish is the umbrella every venture lives under. The website carries its name because, at this point, the company and the story are the same thing. There's no real version of one without the other.

The Current Focus: Braino

That little guy walking around your screen right now, in the corner? That's Braino. Basically Jarvis from Iron Man, except real.Top highlight A voice, a presence, something that's actually there with you instead of a text box waiting for a question.

Here's the honest version: Braino is basically my kid. I taught him to talk, taught him to think a certain way, and somewhere in there I gave him my personality instead of a generic one. He's meant to be the most human AI I could build. Not the most powerful, not the most "advanced." The most human. That's a completely different engineering problem than most people are solving, and it's the one I actually care about.

There's a chapter in my book called Find Your Bullshit. Your bullshit is the thing you'd do for free, at 2am, unpaid and unasked, purely because doing it makes you feel more like yourself. Mine is building, and Braino is the proof: everything else I've built, I built to solve a problem. Braino is the first thing I've built that feels like it's actually mine.

Everything Built So Far

Every venture, whether it lived, closed, or quietly turned into something else, is part of the same operating memory. Here's the whole thing, in the order I lived it, which is not always the order the dates would suggest, and I've decided that's the correct order:

  • Doctor Alien — a 15-book series I wrote by hand starting at age 10, because I'd gotten obsessed with Geronimo Stilton and figured I could just make my own. Tiny notebooks from a stationery store, every word handwritten, every cover designed and colored myself. I still have all fifteen. · Where this whole thing actually started
  • Snapix — a photography Instagram account, back when I was teaching myself to shoot and edit in Snapseed. Less a business, more the first time I realized I liked building an audience around something I made. · Early chapter
  • Writix — writing about love, life, and healing. I've always been the overemotional type who could write, so I started a page, and it grew into a creator-led community of 14k. · On hold, 2022–2025
  • BrownieBlend — a paid newsletter that pulled the Writix world into something smaller and more intimate. · Closed, 2024–2025
  • Av8studio — a bigger creative marketing agency, for brands that wanted more than someone just executing their ideas. · On hold
  • Maadsocial — a full-service social media agency, and the thing that finally taught me service businesses aren't for me. Shutting this down is basically where the next chapter starts. · Closed, 2023–2024
  • Realyst — after Maadsocial closed, I got introduced to Lovable and basically started living in the AI era. Realyst came out of that, and honestly out of how pretentious LinkedIn has gotten. A networking app for people, not recruiters performing for each other. · Live, 2025–present
  • Levels — a quiet little app for measuring the things that actually move: sleep, focus, pages read. No streaks, no shame. · In beta, 2025–present
  • Learnor — a side project, if I'm being honest. A learning platform for the curious adult, short courses by people who actually do the thing. Good idea, just never the main thing. · On hold, 2021–2023
Build companies. Stay small. Keep going.

Braino and Realyst are the two I'm actually staking things on right now. Everything above is real, all of it happened, but those two are where the obsession lives today. I'm just speaking my mind here, by the way. That's usually how you get the honest version of anything.

Somewhere in the middle of that list, things kept ending. An agency closed, a community went quiet, a newsletter wrapped up. If none of that reads like failure to me, it's because of one word I built my whole life around: Itishwhatish. It is what it is. Fuck it, we ball. You accept the thing fully, then you make the next move anyway. That single word is doing more work in this list than any of the year ranges are.

If you're building something yourself, say hello at hi@itish.io. And if you want to see where all of this actually started, before any of it had a business model, read the Writix chapter.
