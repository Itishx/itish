If you've ever wondered what happens when a kid who used to hand-write entire book series grows up and starts companies instead, congratulations. You're already reading it.

Who I Am

I'm Itish Pande, and, the point being, I've been making things since I was about ten years old. I got obsessed with Geronimo Stilton as a kid, decided I could write my own version, and ended up handwriting a fifteen-book series called Doctor Alien. Every word, every cover, done by hand in tiny notebooks from a stationery store. I still have all fifteen.

That's the real starting point, if you want one. Not a business plan. A ten-year-old with a stack of blank notebooks and too many ideas.Top highlight Everything after that, the photography account, the writing page, the agencies, the startups, is the same kid, older, with better tools.

Creatish Inc. is what I ended up calling the umbrella: software, communities, agencies, a book, whatever comes next. One person and one name over all of it. It's intentionally small. Not because small feels safe, but because small stays honest until something actually proves it should be bigger.

Why It's Called Creatish

Creatish isn't a clever brand exercise. It's "create" plus "Itish," and the create part is doing all the work. It's been following me around since the notebooks, and at some point it stopped being a company name and started being a fact about me.

I also just believe it, philosophically: creating is the whole point of being alive. We are born to create. We are born to express. The more you consume, the more depressed you get. The more you create, the happier you get. I wrote a whole book that circles this exact idea, and I have yet to find the exception.

Creatish is the Peter Parker to my Spider-Man. Or the Spider-Man to my Peter Parker, depending on the day. I don't think there's much of a gap left between the guy and the thing he made. I'm Creatish too now, because at my core, that's just what I do. I create.

Ask my friends and they'll tell you I'm basically the grandpa of the group. The dad, the "wise one," whatever you want to call it. They're not wrong, exactly. I'm also, by most accounts, extremely stupid. There's a right balance of when to be the kid and when to be the smart one, and I've landed on this: be the kid almost always, and let the smart part out only when it's about writing or about how I actually live my life. Everywhere else, I'm just here for the nonsense. Staying the kid is a philosophy, by the way, not an accident. It's Key 11 in the book. We'll get there.

How I Work

I do it myself until I physically can't anymore. Writing, design, code, marketing, all of it, one person, no team. Not because I don't think I'll ever need one (I do, and I want my own office and my own crew eventually), but because I've never liked depending on someone else to get something done. Tried that before. Didn't love how it felt. So now I just learn the thing.

Being an only child probably explains half of this. You spend a lot of childhood figuring things out alone, and it sticks. The point being: the self-sufficiency isn't really a personality trait I'm proud of, it's just the default setting I ended up with.

Action creates motivation, not the other way around. That's the one law running underneath all of it, the thing I'd tattoo on the inside of every planner if planners had skin. Everyone waits to feel ready. I've learned to start ugly and let the feeling catch up. It always does. Waiting for motivation before you start is like waiting for balance before you'll agree to pedal.

Everything creative is the same muscle. Handwriting fifteen books, editing photos badly in Snapseed, running a writing page, building software. It's all just me making something out of nothing, badly at first, then less badly. The overlap between all of it is usually where the next real thing shows up.

Build companies. Stay small. Keep going.

Where to Go Next

This site is small on purpose, like the company. Writix holds the writing chapter, the community where the words part started, and the book that grew out of all of it. Creatish holds the startup side, Braino and Realyst, the two I'm actually building right now, and every venture that led here.

And if you're building something yourself, write to hi@itish.io. I usually reply faster than expected.
